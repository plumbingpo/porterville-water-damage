Water Damage We See Most in San Jose

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around San Jose, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and San Jose properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
